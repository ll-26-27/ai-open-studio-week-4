# Shoot: a process or demonstration

A first-year seminar builds looms and weaves on them. The piece shows one student going from bare loom to a finished strip of cloth. There's no interview to lean on, so the pictures alone have to tell the steps in order.

## What coverage means here

- **Every step, in order.** Each stage of the process gets at least one clear shot: setting up, warping, threading, the first passes of the weft, beating it down, the finished cloth. A missing step is a hole the viewer can't jump.
- **Hands and tools up close.** Close-ups and extreme close-ups of the hands, the shuttle, the threads: this is where a process piece lives.
- **Matching action across sizes.** The same motion (a pass of the shuttle) shot wide and close, so the editor can cut on the action from one to the other.
- **Faces, too.** The maker's concentration and reactions, so it's about a person and not only a machine.
- **A before and an after.** The bare loom and the materials at the start; the finished piece, held up, at the end.
- **Where we are.** A wide of the room and the other people working, once, to place the scene.
- **Continuity of the work itself.** The cloth only grows: a strip that's longer in an earlier shot than a later one breaks the story. The same yarn colors throughout, and sleeves and jewelry consistent.
