# Shoot: sit-down interview

A Nieman Fellow interviews a librarian at their desk for a short profile. One camera on the subject, a second angle when we can get it; the interview carries the piece, and everything else exists to cover cuts in what the subject says.

## What coverage means here

- **The subject at three sizes, from the same side.** A medium (waist up), a medium close-up (chest up), and a close-up (face), with the eyeline matching across all three: looking just off-lens toward the interviewer, never into the lens unless that's the style.
- **Consistent framing.** The subject on the same third of the frame in every size, with the space in front of their face (look room) on the interviewer's side.
- **A reverse.** The interviewer from over the subject's shoulder, plus a few nods and listening shots to cut away to (often shot after the interview).
- **A two-shot or a wide** that shows both people and the room, for the start of the piece and for covering a long edit.
- **B-roll of what they talk about.** The subject at work: shelving, at the reference desk, hands on a book, the stacks. At least one sequence (hands, face, wide, over-the-shoulder, unusual angle).
- **Details of the place.** Signage, a nameplate, objects on the desk: inserts that say where we are.
- **Continuity across the sit-down.** Same lighting, glasses on or off throughout, cup and papers where they were, hair and collar unchanged between sizes.
